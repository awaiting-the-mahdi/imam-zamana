Learn step by step

Reading notes & study guides.

These are starter study prompts, not claims about a specific chapter. Exact chapter summaries should be added after checking the text and edition you own.

The Awaited Saviour — starting guide

Focus: belief in the Imam, occultation, and hope for divinely guided justice.

As you read: note the author's central claim, evidence cited, and primary sources. Record chapter titles and page numbers from your own edition.

Reflect: How should hope shape honesty, patience and service to others?

Edition-specific chapter summaries are not yet supplied. Verify chapter titles and page references before publishing them.

Kamaluddin wa Tamamun Ni'ma — starting guide

Focus: an early Imami work important in discussions of the occultation.

As you read: distinguish the author's explanation from reports quoted in the work. Record narrator, wording, chapter and edition details for any narration you share.

Reflect: What evidence is presented, and how does the author explain it?

Do not treat every report in a classical compilation as automatically authenticated.

Al-Ghaybah by Shaykh al-Tusi — starting guide

Focus: reports and scholarly discussion concerning the occultation.

As you read: identify each section's topic and note chains of transmission where provided. Compare translations only when editions and translators are known.

Reflect: Which claims are stated by the source, and which are later interpretations?

Use the precise edition you have before adding page numbers or grading a report.

Study method: turn a chapter into useful notes
  1. Write the exact title and edition details.
  2. Summarise the author's main point in your own words.
  3. List primary sources cited in that chapter.
  4. Separate the author's explanation from direct quotations.
  5. Verify Arabic text and translation before sharing a narration.
  6. End with a practical reflection, labelled as reflection rather than source text.
Read with care

Narrations, supplications & sources.

Spiritual inspiration and careful scholarship belong together. A supplication should not be labelled a hadith, and a narration should not be called authentic without evidence.

Supplication excerpt
اللَّهُمَّ عَرِّفْنِي نَفْسَكَ

“O Allah, make Yourself known to me…”

Opening words of a supplication commonly associated with recognition (ma'rifah).

This is a supplication, not a hadith quotation. Verify the complete wording, attribution and collection reference against a reliable Shia edition before publication.
⌕

How we cite a narration

  1. Original source
    Give the book title and author.
  2. Exact location
    Include volume, chapter, hadith number or page and edition details where available.
  3. Text and translation
    Keep the original Arabic and identify the translation source.
  4. Authenticity note
    Use a reliable scholarly grading when available; otherwise say it has not been independently verified.
No fabricated narrations or “guaranteed result” claims are included. This starter library intentionally uses a short supplication excerpt while its exact source details await verification.
Knowledge into action

Preparation begins today.

These are general ethical reflections for daily life, not a substitute for religious rulings from your marjaʿ.

◇

Strengthen character

Practise truthfulness, fairness, patience and keeping promises—even when nobody is watching.

✧

Serve people

Find a practical way to help family, neighbours or someone who is overlooked.

☾

Make time for learning

Read a little from a reliable source, write down questions and seek sound answers.

۞

Keep a dua routine

Approach supplications with attention and understanding, checking their wording and source.

◎

Seek justice fairly

Reject cruelty and dishonesty in your own choices; do not use justice to justify harm.

✦

Stay hopeful and grounded

Let hope encourage responsibility and compassion rather than fear, rumours or unsupported predictions.

Our purpose

Awaiting the Mahdi is an independent educational project to make learning about Imam al-Mahdi (atfs) approachable through reading guides, source-aware reflections and carefully presented references.

Its aim is to connect knowledge with sincerity, service, justice and hope.

Our source promise

We aim to name sources, distinguish supplications from hadith, identify translation and edition details, and avoid presenting uncertain material as authenticated.

This site is a learning aid, not a substitute for qualified scholarship or a formal religious ruling.
